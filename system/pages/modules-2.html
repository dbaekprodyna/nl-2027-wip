How it works
Synced with the prototype · 7 Oct 2026
The landing page board is the compact form (.r01-compact): position, el-13 at size XS (code only), el-05 at size mini. It now carries its own el-02 switch above the rows (.r01-ctl), because the landing page has no F-04 control slot, and ends with a link to the full table (.r01-foot). Twenty rows; the list is capped (.is-capped).
Note
Three properties carry the unresolved decisions: label holds the Winners-versus-Qualified naming, which is still open; state switches between a live race and a hand-published list, which is the open question about whether qualification is calculated or entered by hand; phase switches in-season to final (ST-10). None of them require a redesign.
Note
R-01 had no Men / Women control of its own (superseded — see below). The switch is a page-level bar directly under the H1 (Alex, deck slide 12 — "Men/women, here in all pages"), so it never moves from one screen to the next and a table header never carries a second one.
R-02 StandingsTable
The Standings page table. Built from el-08 TableHeaderRow and el-04 TeamRow with matched cell widths, and every column is a boolean property on the row so a column change is a switch. It was two views on two tabs — the same federations, ranked the same way, with one column swapped — and it is one table now: ctl-08 ToggleSwitch cuts it down to the twenty places that are going, and the Route column went with the tab because it repeated what the Status marker already says. The switch sits top-left above the table and el-09 Legend top-right, sharing one rule.

E-03 ConferenceGrid

What the Nations League is

Video · 16:9 placeholder
A development competition for players aged 19 to 23, played in conferences of six federations. Every federation that enters does so in both genders, and the season decides who reaches the FIBA 3x3 U23 World Cup.

How qualification works

Twenty federations reach the FIBA 3x3 U23 World Cup: the host, plus nineteen decided across the season. A conference winner qualifies the moment its conference ends. The remaining places go to the highest-ranked federations on tour points.
Places are recalculated after every conference closes
Because conferences finish weeks apart, the race is live from the first tip-off to the last.

Conferences and stops

Federations are grouped into conferences of, in principle, six — geographically where the calendar allows, and within an age group. Each conference is six daily tournaments, its stops, usually played back to back over seven days with a rest day, or in two batches of three.
A stop is won on the day. Where a federation finishes decides what the stop is worth to it, and the conference table is the sum of its stops.

Age categories

The default category is U23 — players aged 19 to 23. A committed federation may register a second team in both genders, and those teams are U21.
Both categories play for the same twenty places, in one global standings per gender: there is no separate U21 competition to qualify from. A federation takes only one place per gender, so a federation that wins two conferences in different age groups qualifies once, and the other place goes to the standings.

Frequently asked questions

When does the season start?
The first conference tips off in June and the last closes in October.
Can a federation enter two teams?
What happens if a conference is cancelled?
